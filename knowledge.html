01 · Start at the beginning

Before the sparkle, there was carbon.

A diamond is essentially carbon arranged in a very special crystal structure. Natural diamonds formed deep inside the Earth under enormous heat and pressure, and some are billions of years old. Geological events eventually brought them closer to the surface, where they could be mined.

That long geological story is what makes a natural diamond fascinating: every stone is a tiny piece of Earth's history.

A GIA image showing rough diamond crystals with different growth histories. Source: GIA.
02 · Two origins, one remarkable material

Natural or lab-grown?
Start with the story, not the sparkle.

Once cut and polished, both can have the same brilliance and the same fundamental diamond properties. The major difference is where and how the crystal grew. GIA and IGI describe laboratory-grown diamonds as diamonds with essentially the same chemical, physical and optical properties as natural diamonds.

NATURAL

Born in the Earth.

Formed deep underground over geological time, then brought upward by natural geological processes. Each stone carries its own natural growth history and inclusions.

LAB-GROWN

Grown by technology.

Made from a diamond seed in a controlled laboratory environment, usually in weeks rather than geological timescales. It is a real diamond — not CZ, not glass, not moissanite.

THE BIG IDEA

Different origin. Same diamond family.

The choice is not “real versus fake.” It is primarily a choice between a naturally formed diamond and a laboratory-grown diamond — with different stories, economics and preferences.

03 · Lab-grown, explained simply

Imagine growing a diamond
instead of digging for one.

Lab-grown diamonds are created using advanced technology. There are two main growth routes: HPHT and CVD. Both begin with carbon and a diamond seed, but they take very different scientific paths.

Natural

Earth's recipe

  • Extreme heat + pressure deep inside Earth.
  • Growth takes an enormous amount of geological time.
  • Volcanic activity can bring diamond-bearing rock toward the surface.
Lab-grown

Technology's recipe

  • A small diamond seed starts the process.
  • Controlled equipment supplies the conditions needed for crystal growth.
  • The rough crystal is then cut and polished like any other diamond.
Quick myth-buster: “Lab-grown” does not mean “fake.” A lab-grown diamond is a diamond. What makes it different from a natural diamond is its origin.
04 · The fun part

CVD vs HPHT —
what's the difference?

Think of them as two different recipes for growing the same extraordinary crystal.

A visual explanation of the two major lab-grown diamond growth methods. Source: Diamore Luraya infographic; technical explanation cross-checked against GIA and IGI.
HPHT

The pressure cooker.

HPHT stands for High Pressure, High Temperature. A carbon source and diamond seed are placed in a growth cell. Extreme pressure and heat help carbon dissolve and crystallize onto the seed.

02Heat + immense pressure

CVD

The microscopic builder.

CVD stands for Chemical Vapor Deposition. A diamond seed sits inside a vacuum chamber. Carbon-containing gas is energized into plasma, and carbon builds onto the seed layer by layer.

One useful trade detail: CVD and HPHT describe the growth process. A CVD-grown diamond can later receive additional treatment, and some lab-grown diamonds may show growth-related features that trained laboratories can identify. So the report and full stone details matter.
05 · The language you will hear everywhere

The 4Cs — your diamond's report card.

Once you know the origin, the next step is understanding quality. The classic 4Cs are Carat, Color, Clarity and Cut. They describe different things — and no single “C” tells the whole story.

C

Carat

Weight, not size. Two diamonds with the same carat weight can look different in face-up size depending on their shape and proportions.

C

Color

For traditional colorless diamonds, the scale generally runs from D (colorless) downward toward warmer grades. Fancy colors are evaluated differently.

C

Clarity

A way of describing internal inclusions and external blemishes. Fewer and less visible features generally mean higher clarity.

C

Cut

How well the diamond's facets interact with light. For a round brilliant, cut has a major influence on sparkle, brightness and fire.

06 · Questions beginners actually ask

No jargon. Just straight answers.

Can I tell natural and lab-grown apart just by looking?

Usually not reliably. Their appearance and many physical and optical properties are very similar. Identification of origin requires appropriate testing and/or a grading laboratory with suitable equipment.

Is HPHT better than CVD?

Not automatically. They are different growth methods. What matters for a particular stone is the finished diamond's quality, treatments, report, proportions, appearance and your requirement.

Does CVD mean a lower-quality diamond?

No. CVD is a growth method, not a quality grade. CVD diamonds can be produced across a range of qualities. Some may receive post-growth treatment, which is why full disclosure matters.

Why does a certificate matter?

A grading report gives an independent description of a diamond's characteristics and, where applicable, identifies whether it is natural or laboratory-grown and may state its growth method or treatments.

Which should I buy — natural or lab-grown?

There is no universal answer. Natural may appeal to someone who values geological rarity and natural origin; lab-grown may appeal to someone who wants a real diamond created by modern technology. Budget, purpose and personal preference all matter.

Yes. You can ask for certified or non-certified options, natural or lab-grown, and specify shape, size, color and clarity. The goal is to show you relevant options rather than overwhelm you with a catalogue.
